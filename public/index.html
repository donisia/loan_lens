    Loan words, explained simply

    Don't let jargon cost you money

    Loan amount (principal)

    The amount the loan agreement is written for. You may receive less than this if fees are taken out first.

    Amount you receive

    The money that actually reaches your phone or account after deductions. Always ask for this number.

    Example: a KES 30,000 loan with a 5% fee taken out gives you KES 28,500.

    Flat rate

    Interest is charged on the full original amount for the whole loan, even after you have repaid most of it. A flat rate costs much more than the same number on a reducing balance.

    10% a month flat on KES 10,000 for 4 months is KES 4,000 in interest.

    Reducing balance

    Interest is charged only on what you still owe, so each payment's interest gets smaller as you repay. Banks and SACCOs usually use this method.

    Processing or facilitation fee

    A charge for arranging the loan. It is often taken from the money you receive, so you pay interest on money you never got.

    Yearly cost rate (APR)

    All interest and fees turned into one yearly percentage, so a 30-day loan and a 12-month loan can be compared fairly. The lower it is, the cheaper the loan.

    Late-payment penalty

    Extra charges if you pay late or miss a payment. Ask exactly how they are calculated, because they can grow quickly.

    Credit life insurance

    Insurance that repays the loan if the borrower dies or becomes disabled. It is a real cost, so ask for the amount.

    Excise duty

    A government tax charged on loan fees in Kenya. It is added on top of fees, so ask whether the quoted fees include it.
